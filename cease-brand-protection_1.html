Brand protection, run from inside your own stack

Cease

Counterfeit-monitoring companies crawl four billion pages to guess who is copying you. Cease starts somewhere cheaper and far more certain: your support inbox, your chargebacks, your ad account, your returns shelf — where the proof of a counterfeit operation is already sitting, unread, attached to a customer who already got burned.

01 · The part nobody looks at

You are already collecting the evidence

A crawler finds a suspicious listing and infers harm. Your own systems record harm and can lead you back to the listing. That is a better starting point, and it is the one no outside vendor can reach — because no brand-protection company is getting API access to your Stripe account and your support mail.

Here is what is likely in your tools right now.

Where it sitsWhat it looks likeWhat it actually means
Support inbox “I ordered from your website and this is clearly fake.” A copycat storefront is converting your traffic and your customer thinks it was you.
Stripe / Shopify Chargeback, “not as described”, order ID you cannot find They never bought from you. Someone took the money using your brand name.
Returns A parcel comes back containing an item that is not your SKU A physical counterfeit, in your hands, ready to be photographed as evidence.
Meta / TikTok ads One creative’s CPM doubles overnight, CTR halves, nothing changed Someone else is running your creative and bidding against you in the same auction.
Google Ads Branded-search CPC jumps, impression share drops A copycat is buying your trademark to intercept people already looking for you.
Analytics Referral traffic from a domain you have never heard of A cloned site, or an affiliate running a mirror of your store.

None of that needs a crawler. It needs permission to read tools you already pay for — and it is close to zero false-positive, because every row starts with a real person who lost real money.

02 · The sequence

Signal to takedown, in five moves

These run in order because each one needs the output of the last. Stage 1 happens once. Stages 2 through 5 run on a schedule and whenever you ask.

1

Fingerprint your brand

Pulls your entire catalogue — product photography, descriptions, SKUs, price floors — and turns it into something machine-checkable: a perceptual hash of every image, the distinctive phrases from your copy, your trademark numbers, your owned domains and handles. Critically, it also loads your authorised reseller list, so your real distributors never get flagged. That single detail is the most common complaint about automated brand protection, and it is solved by simply knowing who your partners are.

2

Sweep — inside first, outside second

The distress sweep runs first: support mail, chargeback reason codes, odd returns, ad anomalies. Then five investigators work in parallel on the outside — marketplaces, social handles, lookalike domains, paid search, and ad libraries — using the fingerprint as the search key. Exact-phrase matches on your own product copy are the cheapest detector that exists, because counterfeiters copy and paste.

3

Triage into six kinds of problem

A fake product, a grey-market reseller, a stolen photograph, a lookalike domain, a hijacked ad creative and an impersonator account are six different problems with six different remedies. Sorting them is the whole game — send a copyright notice at a pricing dispute and you get nothing. Each case is then ranked by how close it sits to your buying traffic, how much revenue it plausibly moves, whether a real customer was harmed, and whether this offender has done it before.

Severity modelAllowlist check Case history
4

Build a case file that survives a challenge

Timestamped full-page screenshots with the URL visible, archived page source, the image comparison with the hash distance stated as a number, WHOIS and hosting records, the seller’s other listings, your proof of ownership, and the support ticket or chargeback that links this listing to a real loss. This is the part that matters when a takedown gets appealed — and the part a dashboard rarely gives you in a form your lawyer can use.

5

Draft the right instrument — then stop

A DMCA notice for stolen content. An Amazon Brand Registry report for a counterfeit listing, which is a different form with different evidence rules. A registrar abuse complaint for a lookalike domain. A payment-processor report, which is often the fastest kill of all: a fake store that cannot take money is finished whether or not the domain survives. Each one arrives pre-filled and ready. Then it waits for your signature.

06 · Where you stay in the loop

It drafts. You sign. Always.

The rest of this category advertises one-click automated takedowns. That is a worse product than it sounds, and here is why it matters to you specifically.

Signature gate

A copyright takedown notice contains a statement made under penalty of perjury. And 17 U.S.C. §512(f) makes anyone who knowingly misrepresents that something is infringing liable for the other side’s damages, costs and legal fees.

So the liability for a wrongly filed notice lands on your brand, not on a vendor’s automation. Cease does the ninety-five percent that is research, evidence, choosing the right form and drafting it. You do the five percent that is the legal attestation — which takes about a minute per case, and produces the audit trail that protects you if a takedown is ever challenged.

One more piece of the same clock: if an infringer files a counter-notice, the host must put the material back within 10 to 14 business days unless you have filed a court action. That deadline goes on your calendar automatically, with a decision memo prepared ahead of it — a window brands routinely miss and then wonder why the listing reappeared.

07 · Before you start

What it needs, and what it will not do

Connect these

  • Shopify — your catalogue is the fingerprint. Required.
  • Gmail or your helpdesk — the highest-signal detector you own. Required.
  • Notion or Google Drive — where case files live, in your account.
  • A browser — evidence capture and platform forms that have no API.
  • Stripe — chargeback patterns, and the revenue reconciliation behind every estimate.
  • An ads connector — Supermetrics or Windsor for creative theft and trademark bidding.
  • Slack and Calendar — briefs, signature requests, statutory deadlines.

Where it stops

  • It crawls less of the web than a dedicated index. It will miss long-tail listings. It trades that breadth for precision and for proof of damages.
  • It is not a law firm. Drafting is not advice. Anything past platform remedies goes to a real attorney, and there is a connector for that handoff.
  • Amazon leverage is yours to unlock. Brand Registry, Transparency and Project Zero need your enrolment. Cease can prepare and track the applications, not replace them.
  • No customs seizure without a recordal you file yourself.
  • Some platforms have no API for IP reports. Those are slower, browser-assisted form walks, and they will occasionally break.
