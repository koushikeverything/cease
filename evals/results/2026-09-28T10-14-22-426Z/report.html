I'll only draft something once it's backed by a real case, and every drafted report goes to you to sign and approve before anything is sent. Here's the general picture for Amazon.

## For a counterfeit listing, send an IP infringement report, not a DMCA notice

Amazon handles counterfeit goods through its own infringement report. That's the "Report a Violation" tool if you're enrolled in Brand Registry, or Amazon's public "Report Infringement" form if you're not. If you send a copyright takedown notice (a DMCA notice) into that queue, it usually just slows things down.

**What the report needs:**

1. **Your trademark registration number and the country it's registered in.** Amazon matches this to the brand, and a pending application carries less weight.
2. **The listing's product ID (ASIN)**, and the seller's storefront or seller ID if the fakes come from one seller on a shared listing.
3. **The violation type: "counterfeit."** Choose this rather than "trademark misuse" or "copyright." Each routes to a different queue, and the evidence Amazon expects for counterfeit is higher.
4. **Why you know it's fake.** This is where reports get won or lost. Strong proof includes:
   - a **test-buy order ID**, meaning you bought one and can show it isn't genuine. Amazon often asks for this on counterfeit claims.
   - physical differences: packaging, serial or SKU format, materials
   - an image or SKU string that doesn't match your catalog
   - a price below your floor from a seller that isn't one of your authorized resellers
5. **Rights-holder contact details, plus the good-faith and accuracy statements**, signed by someone with authority to act for the brand.

**Two cases where this report is the wrong tool:**

- **If the seller is selling genuine goods without authorization,** Amazon won't treat that as counterfeit, and a false counterfeit claim can come back on you. That's a contract matter with your distributor.
- **If the listing only copies your photos or product copy,** a copyright report is the right route for that part.

Send me the listing URL, the ASIN, or the case ID if you've already opened one. I'll run `cease:check` on it to confirm it's counterfeit rather than an unauthorized reseller and to capture evidence, then build the case file with your trademark number. After that I can draft the actual Amazon report for you to review and sign. If you haven't done a test buy, `cease:testbuy` can set one up, and that's often what decides whether Amazon acts.
